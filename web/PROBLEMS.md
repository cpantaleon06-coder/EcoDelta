# Open problems and things not verified

- `meta.json` has no `is_mock`. The DEMO DATA banner shows unless `synthetic` is `false` AND `source` is `"real"`.
- Real data (`ecodelta-engine/web_data/`) does not exist yet. The app has only been checked with the synthetic fixture.
- The fixture's `products` entry is a placeholder (`"not found"`). Real product names, versions and DOIs have not been seen yet.
- Font: Poppins (OFL-1.1) is the only typeface, self-hosted (Latin subset, weights 400, 600 and 700). Characters outside the Latin subset fall back to Arial or Helvetica.
- `maplibre-gl` is BSD-3-Clause, not MIT/Apache-2.0. It is part of the fixed stack, so it stays; listed in THIRD_PARTY.md.
- The Claude desktop app's built-in browser has WebGL turned off, so the map was checked in headless Chromium (Playwright, SwiftShader) instead. Browsers without WebGL now see a message in place of the map.
- Cell details on the map appear on mouse hover or tap only. Keyboard and screen reader users choose regions with the region list, and they cannot read single cells yet.
- The chart's trend line has the engine's slope but no exported intercept. It is drawn through zero at the middle of the period, which fits because the anomalies are relative to the 2001-2025 average. Only the slope carries meaning, and the chart key says so. TODO: ask the engine to export an intercept if a fitted line is wanted.
- On the flat map, polar regions (Arctic, Antarctica) are strongly stretched by Web Mercator. The globe view shows them better.
- The custom accessibility script (`.audit.mjs`) covered text contrast (WCAG AA), sideways overflow at 360, 768, 1024 and 1366 px, and visible keyboard focus. Since design pass v2 it also checks chart text inside the SVG; it does not check the map canvas or screen reader output. No real screen reader was tried.
- Switching variables on the map: see the measurement in the Compare correctness pass below. Not measured on a real GPU.

## World basemap (Natural Earth land, coastlines only)

- Polar clipping: the map uses Web Mercator, which ends at 85.0511° north and south. Cell polygons, region boxes and the graticule are clipped exactly there, and the land polygons (which go to ±90) are clamped there by MapLibre, so cells and coastlines line up. The cell row centered at ±85 reaches the map edge; the rows centered at ±87 and ±89 are not drawn, also not on the globe view (MapLibre's globe is built from the same Mercator tiles and fills the pole caps with the background).
- The pale band at the top of the temperature map in the demo data is real, not a rendering error: the fixture (`ecodelta-engine/src/ecodelta_engine/fixtures.py`, `PLANTED["t2m"]`) adds +0.6 °C per decade to every cell north of 66.5°N on top of +0.25 everywhere. In the fixture's t2m grid the mean slope per row jumps from about 0.22 (60 to 65°N) to about 0.82 to 0.88 (67°N and north), and the color scale ends at ±0.92 (95th percentile of |slope|), so those cells take the top two classes. Sentence for users: "In this demo data set the Arctic north of 66.5°N was given an extra warming trend on purpose, to test the map, so it shows as a pale band with a sharp edge." Real data would not show this hard edge.
- In the fixture, land-only variables use the engine's crude box-shaped land mask, so their cells do not follow the real coastlines on the basemap. Real GLDAS data will.
- Dimmed region boxes (when another region is selected) are drawn at about 1.4:1 against land and ocean, on purpose: they are context only and every region stays available in the region list. Undimmed boxes are about 3:1, coastlines 7.5:1 (land) to 9.4:1 (ocean), with a dark halo so they stay visible over pale data cells.
- The land file adds about 120 KB (gzipped) to the Explore chunk. It is bundled, so the map makes no extra request.

## Compare correctness pass

- Overlapping regions: the old text said the shared cells cancel in the difference. They do not. Each regional mean is divided by its own total area weight, so a shared cell enters D(t) with weight w_i (1/W_A - 1/W_B), which is zero only when W_A = W_B. The text is now corrected in the engine (`COMPARE_METHOD`, `COMPARE_NOTE`), the exported compare files and `meta.methods.region_comparison`, `docs/OUTPUT_SCHEMA.md`, trendkit `METHODS.md` and this page. `tests/test_compare.py::test_shared_cells_do_not_cancel` shows it numerically (A inside B). Pairs with `independent: false` show a smaller, muted verdict and an "Overlapping regions, interpret with caution" badge (icon and text).
- Difference series: the exported `series.anomaly` of each region is the same deseasonalized fixed-mask monthly mean the comparison tests, rounded to 4 significant digits. The browser subtracts A minus B month by month (null when either is null) and plots it with the engine's difference slope, 95% CI and verdict; it computes no statistics. The 12-month line is the difference of the two exported 12-month running means. `python -m ecodelta_engine validate` rebuilds D(t) from the region files for every pair, reruns the trend test and compares it with the compare record (fixture: 990 pairs, 0 mismatches; tolerances 1% of the CI width for slope and CI, 0.01 for p). No new data was exported and no schema field changed.
- Polar cells: Explore now says under the map that cells above about 85 degrees latitude are omitted in Web Mercator, and that the Arctic and Antarctica results are in the region list, the ranking and the detail panels. An end-to-end test opens Antarctica through the region list and the ranking.
- axe (`@axe-core/playwright`, WCAG 2.0 and 2.1 A and AA rules), now `e2e/a11y.spec.ts`, on Explore, Explore with a region and variable chosen (profile and detail panels, Arctic), Compare (overlapping pair) and About: 0 violations. The first run reported color-contrast on 10 muted ranking rows in Explore; they were scanned in the middle of their fade-in animation (opacity below 1). Once settled they pass, so the test now runs with reduced motion, which turns the entrance animations off. No code change was needed. axe does not check the map canvas or whether text makes sense when read aloud. No real screen reader was tried.
- Performance when switching variables (measured, not optimized): production build served locally (`vite preview`, no network latency), headless Chromium with software WebGL (SwiftShader), 1440x900, AMD Ryzen 5 3600, fixture grids of 0.65 to 0.74 MB each. Each switch caused 1 or 2 main-thread long tasks: longest 76 to 219 ms, total 76 to 286 ms per switch, also when the grid was already loaded before. The JS heap rose from 21 MB to about 66 MB after visiting all five variables. The time until the map's loading overlay disappears was 17 to 275 ms, but that number is not reliable: when the grid is ready fast the overlay never appears and the wait ends at once. Not measured on a real GPU, on a phone or over a real network.
